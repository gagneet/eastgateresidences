Payment Plans

Request, approve, and manage formal levy payment plans under the NSW Strata Schemes Management Act 2015

Owners Strata Managers EC Members

What is a payment plan?

If you fall behind on levy payments, you may formally request a payment plan through your building's strata portal. A payment plan is a written agreement between you and the owners corporation that allows you to repay arrears in structured instalments rather than as a single lump sum.

This feature implements the NSW Form 1 payment plan process under sections 83A–83C of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (SSMA 2015). It is available to all buildings on this platform — not just NSW properties.

28-day response window. Under the SSMA 2015, the owners corporation must respond to a payment plan request within 28 days. Your strata manager will be notified immediately when you submit a request.

For owners: how to request a payment plan

1
Go to Dashboard → Finance → Payment Plans.
2
Click Request a Payment Plan. A form will open.
3
Fill in the required details:
  • Unit number — the lot with arrears
  • Total outstanding amount — your current arrears balance in dollars
  • Proposed instalment amount — how much you can pay each period
  • Frequency — weekly, fortnightly, or monthly
  • Proposed start date — when you would like instalments to begin
  • Hardship reason (optional) — a brief statement explaining your circumstances
4
Click Submit Request. The request is immediately visible to your strata manager.
Important: Submitting a payment plan request does not pause levy interest or legal action. Continue making any payments you can while awaiting a decision.

For managers: approving or declining a request

Approving a plan

1
Go to Dashboard → Finance → Payment Plans. Requests with status Requested are pending your action.
2
Click on a plan to open its details. Review the owner's proposed instalment amount, frequency, and hardship statement.
3
Click Approve and complete the approval form:
  • Approved instalment amount — may differ from what the owner proposed
  • Frequency — weekly, fortnightly, or monthly
  • Number of instalments — total count for the plan
  • Start date — when the first instalment is due
  • Internal notes (optional)
4
Click Confirm Approval. The plan moves to Approved status.

Declining a request

If the plan cannot be accommodated, click Decline and provide a written reason (minimum 10 characters). The reason is stored on the record and visible to the owner. The plan moves to Declined status.

28-day deadline. Act on payment plan requests within 28 days of submission. The submission date is shown on every plan record.

Multi-building support

Payment plans are scoped to your building — each building's plans are completely separate from other buildings on the platform. Managers with access to multiple buildings must select the correct building context before viewing or actioning plans.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have more than one active payment plan at a time?
The system allows multiple plan records per unit, but in practice the strata manager will only approve one active plan at a time. Talk to your manager if your circumstances change.
What happens if I miss an instalment?
Missing an instalment does not automatically update the plan — this is a manual process. Contact your strata manager immediately if you cannot make a payment. They may cancel the plan or renegotiate terms.
Can I change the instalment amount after approval?
Approved plans cannot be edited. If you need to change the terms, the current plan can be cancelled and a new request submitted with revised amounts.
What does "hardship reason" mean?
This is an optional written statement explaining your circumstances — for example a job loss, medical expenses, or other financial difficulty. It helps the strata manager assess your request fairly. It is stored securely and only visible to management.
Is the 28-day response requirement mandatory?
Yes. Under the NSW Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (ss.83A–83C), the owners corporation must respond within 28 days. This applies to NSW-based buildings. Other states may have different requirements — check with your strata manager.
Can a tenant request a payment plan?
Tenants are not responsible for levies — that obligation falls on the owner. A tenant experiencing payment difficulty should speak with their landlord.
