Conflict of Interest Register

Declare and record conflicts of interest before Executive Committee agenda items are discussed

EC Members Chairman Strata Managers

What is the conflict of interest register?

The conflict of interest register is a transparent record of situations where an EC member (including the Chairman) has a personal, financial, or professional interest that could — or could be seen to — influence how they participate in a committee decision.

Good governance requires that conflicts are declared before the relevant agenda item is discussed, not after a vote has been taken. Declaring a conflict protects both the individual member and the integrity of the decision.

Transparency rule. This register is visible to all EC members and strata managers for your building. Declarations are permanent records — they cannot be deleted, only retracted by a manager if entered in error.

How to declare a conflict of interest

Timing is critical. Declare your conflict before the agenda item is discussed, not during or after. This ensures the record accurately reflects when the declaration was made.
1
Go to Dashboard → Governance → Conflict of Interest Register.
2
Click Declare a Conflict. A declaration form opens.
3
Complete the form:
  • Conflict type — choose from Financial, Personal, Professional, or Other (see definitions below)
  • Description — explain the nature of the conflict in plain language (minimum 10 characters)
  • Related matter (optional) — enter the agenda item title or motion number this relates to
  • Meeting date (optional) — the date of the meeting
  • Resolution — how the conflict is being handled (see options below). If you are declaring before the meeting, choose Pending
  • Notes (optional) — any additional context
4
Click Submit Declaration. The declaration is immediately recorded with a timestamp and is visible to other EC members and managers.
After the meeting: Once the matter has been dealt with, a manager or chairman can update the resolution field to record the outcome (e.g. "Abstained from voting").

Conflict types explained

Type Plain English meaning Examples
Financial A direct or indirect financial benefit or detriment to you (or a close associate) from the decision You own shares in a contractor being considered for work; you would receive a personal benefit from a levy change
Personal A personal relationship that could influence or appear to influence your judgment The matter involves a family member, close friend, or personal adversary
Professional A conflict arising from your employment, business, or professional role You are employed by a company seeking a contract with the building; your employer has a competing interest
Other Any other situation that a reasonable person might see as a conflict Prior dispute with another party; media conflict; any situation not covered above

Resolution options explained

Resolution Plain English meaning
Pending The conflict has been declared but the matter has not yet been discussed. Select this when declaring before a meeting.
Abstained The member participated in discussion but did not vote on the matter.
Recused The member left the room (or withdrew from the online meeting) for the duration of the discussion and vote.
Disclosed and voted The member declared the conflict, the committee acknowledged it, and the member participated and voted after full disclosure.
Chair ruled no conflict The Chairman ruled that the declared interest does not constitute a conflict requiring abstention or recusal.

Practical example

Scenario: EC member related to a contractor

The agenda for the next EC meeting includes a motion to engage Smith & Sons Plumbing to replace the building's hot water system. EC member Jane Smith's brother is the director of Smith & Sons Plumbing.

What Jane should do:

  1. Before the meeting starts, Jane opens the Conflict of Interest Register and clicks Declare a Conflict.
  2. She selects type Personal and describes: "My brother is a director of Smith & Sons Plumbing, who are being considered for the hot water system contract."
  3. She enters Hot Water System Contractor Selection as the related matter and sets resolution to Pending.
  4. At the meeting, Jane discloses verbally and then leaves the room while the motion is discussed and voted on.
  5. After the meeting, the chairman updates Jane's declaration to Recused.

The declaration is now a permanent, timestamped record in the register.

For managers: updating and retracting declarations

Updating the resolution

After a meeting, the chairman or strata manager can update the resolution field to record how the conflict was handled. Open the declaration from the register list and click Edit Resolution.

Retracting a declaration

If a declaration was entered in error (e.g. wrong meeting, wrong member), a manager can retract it. Retraction is a soft action — the record is marked as retracted but is never deleted. Retracted declarations do not appear in the default register view but can be retrieved by enabling Show Retracted.

Note: Retracting a declaration does not erase it from the audit trail. This is a compliance platform — all records are retained in accordance with the 7-year strata record-keeping requirement.

Multi-building support

Conflict of interest declarations are scoped to your building. Each building has its own register. If you are an EC member of multiple buildings, use the building selector at the top of the dashboard to switch context before viewing or declaring.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to declare a conflict if I'm not sure it's really a conflict?
When in doubt, declare it. An unnecessary declaration is far less problematic than an undisclosed one. The Chairman can rule that no conflict exists after the declaration is made.
Can I edit my declaration after submitting it?
EC members cannot edit their own declarations after submission — this preserves the integrity of the record. If a correction is needed, ask your strata manager to update the notes or resolution field.
What if the Chairman has a conflict?
The Chairman must declare the conflict the same as any other EC member. The Vice-Chairman or another EC member nominated by the committee takes the chair for that agenda item.
Is this register required by law?
Yes. Disclosure of conflicts is a legal obligation for strata committee members under strata legislation. Maintaining a written register demonstrates compliance and protects all parties in any dispute.
Who can see the register?
All EC members and strata managers for your building can see the register. Owners and tenants cannot access it directly, but the minutes of meetings (which record conflicts) may be available to owners.
