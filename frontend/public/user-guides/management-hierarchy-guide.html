Management Hierarchy Guide

This guide explains how strata management is structured on StrataOS — and how you can see who is responsible for your building. It is written for strata managers, EC members, and owners.

How Strata Management Is Organised

On StrataOS, strata management follows a simple four-level structure:

  1. Strata Management Company — the firm that holds your strata management contract (for example, "Acme Strata").
  2. Management Entity — a team or office within that company. A large company may have several offices or departments, each handling a set of buildings.
  3. Building Assignment — your building is assigned to one management entity. This determines which team handles day-to-day management.
  4. Appointed Strata Manager — the individual strata manager within the entity who is responsible for your building. This is the person you deal with directly.
In plain terms: Think of it as a company, then a branch office, then your building, then your dedicated manager. Each level rolls up into the one above it.

Who Manages Your Building?

To see who is currently appointed as the strata manager for your building:

  1. Log in to the portal.
  2. Go to Building Info or Settings → Building Details.
  3. Look for the Management section. This shows the management company name, the management entity, and the name and contact details of your appointed strata manager.

EC members and owners can view this information. You cannot change it from this screen — changes to the manager appointment are made by the strata admin.

Important: Only one strata manager is appointed to your building at any time. If multiple people from the management company contact you, your appointed manager is the primary point of contact for formal matters.

Requesting a Change of Manager

The EC has the authority to request a change of strata manager. The process is:

  1. Raise the matter at an EC meeting and pass a resolution.
  2. Contact the strata management company directly, quoting the resolution.
  3. If the company re-appoints a different individual, the strata admin will update the appointment in StrataOS. The new manager's name and contact details will appear automatically in your portal.
  4. If you are changing companies entirely, the incoming company's strata admin will set up the new management structure and invite your EC members to confirm the new arrangement.
Tip: You will receive a notification in the portal when your appointed strata manager changes. Check your notification bell if you heard about a change but have not seen it reflected yet.

What This Means for EC Members

As an EC member, understanding the hierarchy helps you:

  • Know exactly who to escalate issues to if your day-to-day manager is unresponsive.
  • Understand which team handles your building's records, finances, and compliance.
  • See the management entity's portfolio — so you know which other buildings share the same management team (useful when comparing service levels).
  • Request that the strata admin reassign your building to a different entity within the same company if the service is poor.

What This Means for Owners

As an owner, the hierarchy is mostly handled behind the scenes. What matters to you is:

  • Your building always has one named strata manager. Their contact details are visible in Building Info.
  • Your levies, maintenance requests, and documents are all managed under that appointment.
  • If you believe a management issue is not being addressed, your first step is to raise it at an EC meeting or contact your EC chairperson.
