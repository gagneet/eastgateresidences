EC Member BI Analytics Guide

This guide is for Executive Committee members. It explains how to access and read the BI Analytics dashboard for your building — and what to do when something looks wrong.

You do not need any financial or technical background to use these screens. The analytics are designed to give you a clear picture of your building's health at a glance.

How to Access the Analytics Page

  1. Log in to the portal.
  2. From the left-hand sidebar, go to Dashboard → Analytics.
  3. The analytics page loads showing data for your building. If you are an EC member on more than one building, use the building switcher in the top navigation to select the correct building.
Tip: You can bookmark the Analytics page for quick access. The data refreshes automatically — you do not need to do anything to keep it up to date.

Understanding the 14 Panels

The analytics page is divided into 14 panels, grouped by theme. Here is what each one tells you:

Levy and Finance Panels

  • Levy Collection Rate — the percentage of levies billed this quarter that have actually been paid. A healthy building is usually above 90%.
  • Arrears Total — the total dollar amount owed to the building by lot owners who have not paid their levies. Click this panel to see a breakdown by lot.
  • Admin Fund Balance — how much money is currently in the day-to-day operating account. Compare this to the budget to see if the building is on track.
  • Sinking Fund Projection — a forecast of whether the sinking fund will have enough money to cover upcoming major works (such as painting, lifts, or roofing). Green means adequately funded; red means a top-up levy or special levy may be needed.
  • Budget vs. Actual — compares this year's approved budget with actual spending to date, by category. Over-budget categories are highlighted.

Maintenance Panels

  • Open Maintenance Requests — how many work orders are currently open across your building. A high number that is not trending down may indicate the manager needs more resources.
  • Maintenance Costs — total spend on maintenance and repairs this financial year, compared to the maintenance budget line.
  • SLA Performance — the percentage of maintenance requests resolved within the agreed timeframe. Below 80% is worth raising with your strata manager.

Compliance Panels

  • Compliance Status — a count of compliance obligations (fire safety, essential services, certificates) that are current, due soon, or overdue.
  • Upcoming Compliance Deadlines — a list of compliance items due in the next 90 days so you can plan ahead.

Occupancy and Community Panels

  • Occupancy Mix — the breakdown of your building by owner-occupiers, investors (tenanted lots), and vacant lots. Useful for understanding your community profile.
  • Active Owners — the percentage of owners who have logged in to the portal and engaged with notices or votes in the past 90 days.
  • Maintenance Request Volume Trend — a line chart showing whether maintenance requests are increasing or decreasing over time. A rising trend may indicate building deterioration or seasonal issues.
  • Financial Health Score — a single composite score (0–100) summarising the overall financial health of your building, combining arrears, fund balances, collection rate, and budget adherence.

What the RAG Status Colours Mean

Many panels show a RAG status — Red, Amber, or Green:

  • Green — within acceptable range. No action needed from the EC.
  • Amber — approaching a threshold that needs monitoring. Discuss at your next EC meeting.
  • Red — outside acceptable range. This warrants a conversation with your strata manager as soon as possible.
Important: Red does not always mean an emergency. It means the figure is outside the normal healthy range and deserves attention. Your strata manager should already be aware of red items — but it is reasonable to ask for an update.

Raising a Concern Based on Analytics Data

If you see a red or amber figure that concerns you, here is the recommended approach:

  1. Screenshot or note the panel — click the panel to see more detail before raising the concern.
  2. Check the trend — is the figure getting better or worse? A number that is amber but improving is less urgent than one that is red and worsening.
  3. Contact your strata manager — use the contact details in Building Info. Quote the panel name and the figure you are concerned about.
  4. Raise it at an EC meeting — for matters that need a formal resolution (such as approving a special levy or engaging a debt collection agency), put it on the EC meeting agenda.
Tip: The analytics page has a Download Report button. You can generate a PDF summary of the current analytics to share with fellow EC members before a meeting.
