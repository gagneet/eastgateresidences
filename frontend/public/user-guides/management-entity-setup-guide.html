Management Entity Configuration Guide

This guide is for Strata Admins. It explains what a management entity is, how to create one, and how to use entities to organise your portfolio of buildings into logical groups.

What Is a Management Entity?

A management entity is a team or office within your strata management company. Think of it as a sub-division of your company that is responsible for a specific group of buildings.

For example, a company with offices in multiple locations might have:

  • One entity for the Canberra office, managing ACT buildings.
  • One entity for the Sydney office, managing NSW buildings.
  • One entity for a specialist commercial team, managing commercial strata buildings.

Entities help you organise reporting, assign managers efficiently, and track performance by team — rather than looking at all buildings as one undifferentiated list.

Tip: If your company is small with one office, you only need one management entity. You can always add more entities later as your portfolio grows.

Creating a Management Entity

  1. Log in as a strata admin and go to Admin → Management Entities.
  2. Click New Entity.
  3. Enter a name for the entity (for example, "ACT Residential" or "Northern Suburbs Office").
  4. Optionally, enter a description and a team lead contact name.
  5. Click Create Entity.

The entity is created immediately. It will appear in your entities list and in the building assignment screen.

Assigning Buildings to the Entity

After creating an entity, assign buildings to it:

  1. Open the entity from Admin → Management Entities.
  2. Go to the Buildings tab.
  3. Click Add Buildings and select the buildings you want to assign to this entity from your company's portfolio list.
  4. Click Save.

A building can only belong to one entity at a time. If you need to reassign a building to a different entity (for example, after a team restructure), simply move it from one entity to another — no data is lost in the process.

Important: Reassigning a building to a new entity does not change its appointed strata manager. If you also want to change the manager, do that separately from the building's Management tab.

Appointing Managers to Buildings Within the Entity

Once buildings are assigned to an entity, you can appoint individual strata managers to each building:

  1. Open the entity and go to the Buildings tab.
  2. Click on a building name to open its management detail.
  3. Go to the Manager Appointment section.
  4. Select the strata manager from your company's staff list. Only managers who are members of your company appear in this list.
  5. Enter the contract start date and confirm the appointment.

The appointed manager immediately sees the building in their portfolio and can begin work. Owners and EC members in the building will see the manager's name and contact details update automatically.

Tip: A strata manager can be appointed to buildings in different entities — for example, if a manager covers buildings across two offices. The manager sees all their buildings in one portfolio view regardless of which entity each building belongs to.

Viewing Entity-Level Analytics

Each entity has its own analytics view, which aggregates performance data for all buildings in the entity. Access it from Admin → Management Entities → [Entity Name] → Analytics.

The entity analytics view shows:

  • Portfolio health — the distribution of green, amber, and red buildings within this entity.
  • Levy collection rate — the average collection rate across all buildings in the entity for the current quarter.
  • Arrears total — the combined outstanding levy balance for buildings in this entity.
  • Compliance status — how many compliance items are current, due soon, or overdue across the entity's buildings.
  • Maintenance SLA performance — average resolution time and breach count across the entity.
  • Manager workload — a breakdown of how many buildings each manager in the entity is responsible for, and their individual performance metrics.

Use this view for team-level performance reviews and to identify whether a manager is overloaded or whether specific buildings need a change in approach.
