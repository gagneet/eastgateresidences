Owner Investment Analytics Guide

This guide is for lot owners who want to understand the financial health and long-term value of their investment. Your owner dashboard includes an investment analytics view that goes beyond your levy balance — it gives you a picture of the building's overall condition and what it means for your lot.

Good to know: This information is for your reference and does not constitute financial advice. For advice specific to your situation, speak with a financial adviser or conveyancer.

Your Total Cost of Ownership

The Total Cost of Ownership section shows everything charged to your lot in the current financial year:

  • Admin fund levies — your contribution to the building's day-to-day running costs (cleaning, insurance, utilities, management fees, etc.).
  • Sinking fund levies — your contribution to the long-term capital works fund (future painting, lift replacements, roofing, etc.).
  • Special levies — any one-off charges raised by the owners corporation for unexpected works or shortfalls.
  • Water charges — if your building charges water separately, this appears as its own line.
  • Total year-to-date — the sum of all of the above for this financial year.

Your lot's share of each fund is calculated using your unit of entitlement — a number set in the registered strata plan that reflects the relative value and size of your lot compared to others in the building.

Understanding the Yield Estimate for Your Lot

If your lot is tenanted (or you are considering renting it out), the Yield Estimate panel shows:

  • Estimated gross yield — annual rent as a percentage of the estimated property value. This is indicative only and is based on publicly available data for comparable properties in your area.
  • Estimated net yield — gross yield minus your annual levy costs and estimated management fees. This gives you a more realistic picture of your return.
Important: Yield estimates are based on general market data and your actual levies. They do not account for your mortgage, depreciation, tax, or other personal costs. Always get independent advice before making investment decisions.

Capital Works Schedule and Your Levies

The Capital Works Schedule shows the building's planned major works over the next 10 years and what they mean for your levies:

  • Each planned work item is listed with its estimated cost and target year.
  • Your lot's share of each item (based on your unit of entitlement) is shown alongside the total cost.
  • If the sinking fund is projected to be insufficient, the schedule will show a funding gap — this is the amount that may need to be raised through increased sinking fund levies or a special levy.

Use this view to plan ahead for future costs. A building with a well-funded sinking fund and a realistic capital works schedule is generally a lower-risk investment.

Your Building's Health from an Investment Perspective

The Building Health Score panel gives you a simplified view of the building's overall financial condition:

  • High score (70–100) — the building is well-managed, levies are being collected, funds are healthy, and maintenance is up to date. Good for property values.
  • Medium score (40–69) — some areas need attention. Review the detail to understand which aspects are underperforming.
  • Low score (0–39) — the building has significant challenges. This could affect resale value and may indicate the need for EC intervention.

The score is made up of: levy collection rate, sinking fund adequacy, arrears level, compliance status, and maintenance responsiveness.

What to Do If Your Building Has a High Risk Score

A high building risk score does not mean you need to sell. Here are the steps to take:

  1. Read the detail — click on the risk score to see which specific areas are contributing to it. A single overdue compliance item is very different from a sinking fund shortfall combined with high arrears.
  2. Contact an EC member — your building's executive committee is responsible for oversight. Raise your concerns with a committee member or at the next general meeting.
  3. Attend the next AGM — the annual general meeting is where key decisions about levies, capital works, and management are made. Your vote matters.
  4. Consider your options — if the situation is serious, consult a strata lawyer or your conveyancer for advice on your rights as an owner.
Tip: You can download a Building Investment Summary PDF from your owner dashboard. This document summarises your lot's costs, the building's health score, and the capital works schedule — useful for your accountant or financial adviser at tax time.
