Owner Resident Handbook

Welcome to your comprehensive guide as a property owner at your building. This guide helps you navigate the platform to manage your property, stay informed, and engage with the community.

Quick Start Checklist
  • Complete your profile and set directory privacy preferences.
  • Check the Notice Board for recent building updates.
  • Review Levy Status to ensure your accounts are up to date.
  • Familiarize yourself with the Document Library for bylaws and meeting minutes.

Registration & Building Selection

When creating your account at /register, the platform will display all available buildings. Select your building (e.g. your building — your building's address), then choose Owner as your role and enter your unit number.

  • If only one building is available, a banner will confirm your building automatically.
  • If multiple buildings are listed, click the card for your building before proceeding.
  • After login, if your account belongs to one building, the platform switches to the correct building context automatically (no manual step required).
  • Super Admins and users in multiple buildings will see a Switch Building option in the header.

Self-Service Requests

Submit and track formal requests to the Owners Corporation at Dashboard → Requests.

  • Insurance Claim: Lodge a new insurance claim (water damage, fire, theft, storm). Upload photos, select incident type and date, provide a description, and track claim status through the review process.
  • Insurance Enquiry: Ask questions about your coverage, claim procedures, or request a Certificate of Currency. The response timeframe is set by the building team.
  • Access Control: Request review for a building access device or replacement. The building team confirms eligibility, next steps and any applicable charges.
  • Alterations: Request EC approval for any renovation or modification to your unit. Provide contractor details, proposed start date, upload plans, and acknowledge By-Law 7. Processing takes up to 30 days.
  • Reimbursement: Claim back approved out-of-pocket expenses (e.g. emergency repairs). Attach receipts and provide bank account details (BSB + account number) for review. Finance approval and payment handling depend on your building's workflow permissions.

Governance

  • Committee Meetings: View upcoming meeting dates, agendas, and previous minutes.
  • Voting & AGM: Cast your vote on important motions during the Annual General Meeting or other ballots.

  • Compliance Checklist: Monitor the building's compliance status with safety and regulatory requirements.
  • Unit Plan: Access the official registered units plan (your unit plan), including floor plans and unit entitlements.

Financial Transparency: These tools are designed to help you make informed decisions during AGMs regarding budget approvals and levy contributions.

Advanced Financial Enhancements

Understanding Your Financial Summary Card

The Financial Summary card on your Owner Dashboard shows two key figures: Current Balance and Next Estimated Payment. Both figures automatically account for all carry-forward credits, outstanding arrears, and any advance payments you have already made during the year.

Current Balance

This is the net amount you currently owe (shown in amber/red) or have in credit (shown in green). It reflects your opening carry-forward from the prior financial year combined with this year's levied amounts minus everything you have paid so far.

Next Estimated Payment

This is the amount you need to pay at the next due date to bring your account current. It is calculated as follows:

  • Prior-year credit — if you overpaid last year, that credit is automatically applied to reduce your next instalment. For example, if you have a $200 credit and the quarterly levy is $1,400, the card will show $1,200 (not $1,400). A green label will confirm: "$200 prior-year credit applied".
  • Prior-year arrears — if you carried a balance from last year, it is folded into the current period's obligation. For example, $200 arrears + $1,400 quarterly levy = $1,600 outstanding for the current period.
  • Advance or partial payments — any confirmed payments you have made this year are credited immediately. If you paid $1,000 toward a $1,400 quarterly levy, the card shows $400 remaining.
  • Multi-period advance — if you paid more than one full instalment in advance (e.g. $1,800 against a $1,400 quarterly levy), the extra $400 is applied to the next period. The card advances the due date accordingly.

After the grace period (14 days past due date)

Once a payment period passes its due date plus the 14-day grace period, any outstanding amount from that period becomes overdue arrears. The card will then show:

  • The next period's full levy amount plus the overdue arrears from the missed period.
  • A red sub-label: "Includes overdue amount from previous period".
  • The next due date advances to the following period's due date.
Example: Credit $200 (from prior year) + paid $1,000 this year, quarterly levy $1,400.
  • Before 31 March + 14 days: Next Estimated Payment = $200 · Next Due: 31 March.
  • After 14 April: Next Estimated Payment = $1,600 ($1,400 Q2 levy + $200 Q1 arrears) · Next Due: 1 June.
Tip: Payments recorded via DEFT or BPAY outside the portal will appear once your strata manager verifies them. Until verified, the card may show a higher "Next Estimated Payment" than expected. Contact the strata manager if your payment is not reflected within 5 business days of payment.

Levy Due Dates and the Financial Year

Levy due dates are set by building management in Site Settings. The default schedule is quarterly, with payments due in September, December, March and June.

Because the ACT strata financial year runs from July to June, the schedule spans two calendar years. The September and December payments belong to the start of the financial year (e.g. September 2025 and December 2025 for FY2025-26), while the March and June payments belong to the end of the financial year (e.g. March 2026 and June 2026 for FY2025-26).

  • Grace period: A 14-day grace period applies after each due date. You will not be considered overdue until 14 days after the due date.
  • Days overdue: Counted from the most recent past grace deadline — i.e. from the day after your 14-day grace expired.
Example (FY2025-26): Default quarterly due dates: 30 Sep 2025, 31 Dec 2025, 31 Mar 2026, 30 Jun 2026.
Grace deadlines: 14 Oct 2025, 14 Jan 2026, 14 Apr 2026, 14 Jul 2026.
As of 16 March 2026: Q1 (14 Oct) and Q2 (14 Jan) deadlines have passed — any unpaid Q1/Q2 levies are overdue. Q3 (14 Apr) grace has not yet passed.

OwnerHub — Landlord & Property Management

OwnerHub is your integrated landlord platform. Whether you manage your your building\'s unit as a rental property, or you own other investment properties elsewhere, OwnerHub gives you a single place to track everything.

OwnerHub at a Glance
  • My Properties — Manage your building\'s units and external investment properties.
  • Tenancies & Leases — Track lease dates, bond details, rent amounts and renewal status.
  • Rent Ledger — Record and review all rent payments, identify arrears.
  • Inspections — Schedule entry, routine and exit inspections.
  • Property Health Score — Weekly investment health score (0–100).
  • True Cost of Ownership — See the real profit/loss including capital works and vacancy.

Managing Your Properties

Navigate to Dashboard → OwnerHub → My Properties. You can add your your building\'s unit (link it to the strata unit number for automatic levy data) or add external investment properties. Each property card shows occupancy status, current tenant, lease expiry, and weekly rent.

Tenancies & Leases

Go to Dashboard → OwnerHub → Tenancies & Leases. Here you can create and manage lease agreements, track bond lodgement numbers, and receive renewal alerts when a lease is within 90 days of expiry.

The arrears engine automatically flags overdue rent and tells you which stage applies:

  • Reminder — 3 days overdue
  • Warning Notice — 7 days overdue
  • Breach Notice — 14 days overdue
  • Tribunal Preparation — 21+ days overdue

Rent Ledger

Record every rent payment from Dashboard → OwnerHub → Rent Ledger. The ledger shows payment date, amount, method (bank transfer, BPAY, DEFT), reference number and status. Export the ledger as a CSV for your accountant at end of financial year.

Inspections

Schedule and document routine, entry and exit inspections from Dashboard → OwnerHub → Inspections. Each inspection record captures the date, inspector, condition score (1–10), and can hold a report URL or uploaded photos.

Property Health Score

Navigate to Dashboard → OwnerHub → Property Health Score. Your score (0–100) is calculated weekly across six components:

Component Weight What it measures
Income Stability 25% Rent payments, arrears history
Expense Volatility 15% Unexpected maintenance cost spikes
Maintenance Backlog 20% Open/pending repair requests
Tenant Stability 15% Lease tenure, renewal risk
Capital Risk 15% Asset age, upcoming major works
Compliance 10% Safety checks, inspection recency

A score of 75+ is Healthy (green). 50–74 is Moderate (yellow). 30–49 is High Risk (orange). Below 30 is Critical (red).

True Cost of Ownership

Navigate to Dashboard → OwnerHub → True Cost of Ownership. This calculator shows the real annual profit or loss for your investment, including:

  • Gross rental income
  • Vacancy allowance
  • Strata levies (auto-populated for your building\'s units)
  • Maintenance costs (from your repair history)
  • Capital works unit share (from building forecast)
  • Financing costs (mortgage interest)

A 10-year cashflow projection chart shows how your investment performs over time, including any years where major capital works will reduce cashflow.

Important: Enter your mortgage balance and interest rate to get accurate financing costs. These figures are stored locally and not shared with other users.

Building Financial Stress Early Warning

Navigate to Dashboard → Financials → Building Financial Stress. This unique feature predicts whether the building may require a special levy in the coming years, giving you time to prepare financially.

The system calculates the Reserve Adequacy Ratio (RAR):

RAR = Sinking Fund Balance ÷ 10-Year Capital Works Forecast

A low RAR (below 0.4) indicates risk. The dashboard also shows:

  • Overall Risk Score (0–100) with stress level badge
  • 10-year reserve projection chart (showing the year the fund may go into deficit)
  • Your unit's estimated special levy if the shortfall is not addressed
  • Three mitigation options (increase sinking levy / defer works / strata loan)
This data is refreshed monthly. As an owner, use this information to engage with the Executive Committee about proactive financial planning.

Smart Request — Submit & Track Queries

Navigate to Dashboard → Smart Request or Dashboard → Requests.

Use the 3-step Smart Request tool instead of emailing the strata manager. The system classifies your request automatically and sets an SLA response window.

  1. Step 1 — Category: Choose from Maintenance Issue, Levy Query, By-Law Query, Document Request, Noise Complaint, or Other.

Your Building Intelligence Hub — Dashboard v2 (May 2026)

Your dashboard has been redesigned with more personalised, actionable information about your building and your unit. Every section described below pulls from real data — nothing is fabricated.

This Week — Only Your Stuff

Up to four cards showing the actions that require your attention this week:

  • Due soon (amber): Levy quarters overdue or due within 14 days.
  • Vote open (indigo): An AGM or proposal where your vote is still open.
  • Action needed (rose): An open maintenance request awaiting your reply, or an overdue capital project update.
  • Update (slate): Informational notices about your building.

When you have nothing outstanding the card reads "Nothing waits for you this week." — this is the goal.

Your Building's Strength

A ring gauge (0–100) showing your building's overall health across four dimensions:

  • Funds healthy: Whether the sinking fund stays positive through the 10-year forecast.
  • Compliance: Percentage of statutory certificates that are current.
  • Maintenance SLA: Whether any maintenance requests have breached their SLA in the last 30 days.
  • Insurance: Whether an active building insurance policy is on file and when it next renews.

True Cost of Ownership

A 12-month breakdown of every dollar you pay to hold your unit — strata levies, council rates, water charges, building insurance apportionment, land tax, and utilities. The stacked bar shows relative proportion; each tile below shows the annual dollar amount. A year-on-year delta indicates whether your total cost rose or fell compared with last year.

Market Intelligence

Up-to-date market context for your suburb and unit:

  • Estimated market value and suburb median, with year-on-year growth percentage.
  • Rental yield and average days on market.
  • Building premium — the percentage by which your building trades above or below the suburb median.
  • A 7-year value trend sparkline and up to three recent comparable sales in your area.

Your Requests & Emergency Contacts

Your three most recent open maintenance or enquiry requests are shown with their status:

  • Needs your reply (rose): The manager or contractor has responded and is awaiting your input.
  • Overdue (amber): The request has passed its SLA due date.
  • In progress (amber): Being actively worked on.

Below the requests panel, the emergency contacts card lists after-hours numbers (building manager, lift emergency, on-call plumber, etc.) so you never have to hunt for them.

Weekly Reminders

The "This week" section header shows Reminders on · Mon 9am. A digest email is sent every Monday morning summarising your outstanding actions. Adjust or opt out via Dashboard → Notification Settings.
