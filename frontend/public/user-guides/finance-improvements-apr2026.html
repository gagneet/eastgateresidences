Finance Feature Updates — April 2026

This guide covers all finance-related improvements made to the Strata Management platform in April 2026, addressing 9 reported issues. These changes affect the Collection Rate page, Financial Transactions tab, Finance Intelligence page, Financial Projections, and Investor Intelligence.

1. Collection Rate vs Fund Health — Consistency Fix

Fixed

Previously, the Collection Rate percentage and Fund Health percentage on the finance dashboard showed different values (e.g., 63.09% vs 64.2%). This caused confusion about the building's actual levy collection performance.

⚠️ Superseded (Aug 2026 — GAP-FIN-040): The April-2026 change described in this section made Fund Health reuse the Collection Rate calculation. That equivalence has since been reversed. Fund Health / Full-Year Levy Coverage (measured against the full year's levy, including instalments not yet due) and the due-date Collection Rate (measured only against levies due so far) are now two distinct metrics that are never equated or labelled interchangeably — alongside a third, "Collected in Advance". The account below is retained as historical context; for current definitions see the finance tech-docs and the repository changelog.

What Changed

The Fund Health metric was changed (April 2026) to use the same calculation as Collection Rate — the weighted average of all levies collected across the entire building. Previously it used an unweighted average of the admin fund rate and sinking fund rate separately, which diverges when the two funds have different sizes. (This equivalence was later reversed — see the superseded note above.)

Metric Before After
Collection Rate total_paid / total_levied total_paid / total_levied (unchanged)
Fund Health (admin_rate + sinking_rate) / 2 (unweighted) total_paid / total_levied (weighted, same as Collection Rate)
Individual fund rates (admin and sinking separately) are still shown in the detailed breakdown panels for transparency.

2. Collection Rate Page — Numbers Now Add to 100%

Fixed

The Collection Rate analysis page previously showed 4 metrics (Overall Rate, Total Collected, Total Outstanding, Not Paid Due Date) that did not add up to 100% of the total levied amount. The missing portion (~24-25%) was future quarterly instalments not yet due.

Clarification (GAP-FIN-040): a breakdown bar that adds to 100% of the full-year levy — with "Not Yet Due" as one of its segments — is the Full-Year Levy Coverage view, which is distinct from the due-date Collection Rate. The Collection Rate excludes not-yet-due instalments from both its numerator and denominator, so it is not the same number as the 100% coverage bar and is never labelled the same.

New Breakdown Bar

The page now shows a colour-coded progress bar with all four components that always add to 100%:

Segment Colour Meaning
Collected Green Confirmed payments received
In Grace Period Amber Past due date but within 14-day processing window — may still arrive
True Arrears Red Genuinely overdue with no grace window remaining
Not Yet Due Grey Future quarterly instalments not yet at their due date
Example: If the building raises $109,000 in annual levies and only Q1 and Q2 have fallen due by March, approximately 50% of the levy is "Not Yet Due" (Q3 and Q4). This is normal and expected.

3. Finance Transactions Tab — Now Shows Real Transactions

Fixed

The Transactions tab on the main Finance page previously showed the same Unit Levy Ledger data as the Levy Status tab. It now displays actual income and expense transactions recorded in the system.

What You'll See

  • Income transactions — interest earned, grants, other income (shown in green)
  • Expense transactions — insurance, utilities, management fees, maintenance (shown in red)
  • Each transaction shows: Date, Type, Description, Category, Fund (Admin/Sinking), Amount
  • Sorted by most recent first
This is where you can track exactly where the building's levy money is being spent and what income the building is generating.

Fixed

What Changed

  • The year is loaded dynamically from available financial records
  • Creating projections now works correctly

Financial Year Model — How It Works

What Is the Building Financial Year?

The building has its own financial year, independent of the ATO tax year (July–June). your building uses a calendar year: FY 2026 runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026.

The financial year start month is configured in Site Settings → Financials. If your building's FY starts in January, the label is simply "FY 2026". If it starts in any other month (e.g. July), the label spans two years: "FY 2025-26".

Levy Due Dates

Levy instalments for FY 2026 at your building are due on:

Quarter Due Date
Q1 31 March 2026
Q2 1 June 2026
Q3 1 September 2026
Q4 1 December 2026

These dates are set in Site Settings → Financials → Levy Due Custom Dates.

GST on Levies

your building is GST-registered (annual turnover exceeds the $75,000 ATO threshold). Levies include GST. The figures work as follows:

  • Administrative Fund budget (ex-GST): $309,882.00
  • Sinking Fund budget (ex-GST): $90,459.00
  • Total budget (ex-GST): $400,341.00
  • GST (10%): $40,034.10
  • Total levy collection (inc-GST): $440,375.10

Each unit's quarterly levy already includes the GST component. The unit levy amount shown on statements is the GST-inclusive amount. GST is reported to the ATO via quarterly BAS by your strata manager.

Administrative Fund vs Sinking Fund

  • Administrative Fund: Covers day-to-day operating expenses (cleaning, insurance, electricity, management fees, etc.). The levy collected approximately matches the annual budget.
  • Sinking Fund: Long-term capital reserve. Funds accumulate year-on-year to cover large future expenses (e.g. painting, lift overhaul, major repairs). The sinking fund levy is not intended to equal current-year expenses — it builds up over time.

Note: Your building may use different fund terminology — the correct term here is Sinking Fund, which is consistent with ACT Unit Titles legislation.

Finance Year-Linkage Fixes (April 2026 — Update 2)

The following bugs were identified and fixed as part of the April 2026 second update:

1. Overview Tab — Historical Years Showing $0.00

Bug: The Overview tab was displaying $0.00 for all financial metrics when selecting any year from 2021 to 2025. Only the current year (2026) was showing correct figures.

Root cause: The backend model (UnitLevyLedgerResponse) required four fields (id, lot_number, uoe, property_type) that were absent from historical database records (seeded before these fields were introduced). The API returned HTTP 500, causing the frontend to silently zero all cards.

What was fixed:

  • Fields made optional in the response model — historical records no longer cause a 500 error.
  • All historical records (2021–2025, 435 documents) backfilled with correct values:
    • uoe (Unit of Entitlement) sourced from the units collection
    • property_type sourced from units.unit_type (TH* = townhouse, UA* = apartment)
  • Frontend data-fetching updated to be resilient — a single endpoint failure no longer zeroes all tabs.

2. Quarterly Trend Chart — Static / Identical Lines

Bug: The "Monthly Trend" chart on the Overview tab showed two overlapping lines (Income and Levies were always identical).

What was fixed:

  • The Levies line now shows the budgeted quarterly levy (annual budget ÷ 4).
  • The Income line now shows actual levy payments collected per quarter from the payment records database.
  • The chart is now renamed "Quarterly Trend" for accuracy.
  • Selecting a different Financial Year updates the chart to show that year's data.

How to read it: When the Income line is below the Levies line, some levies are outstanding for that quarter. When they match, collection is on track. Future quarters will show a flat Income line until payments are recorded.

3. Levy Status Tab — Not Responding to Year Change

Bug: The Levy Status tab was showing the same unit list regardless of which Financial Year was selected in the dropdown.

What was fixed:

  • The Levy Status tab now sources its data directly from the year-filtered levy ledger records.
  • Amounts for Total Levied, Total Paid, and Balance now reflect the selected Financial Year.
  • Owner names are still shown (enriched from unit records), while financial figures come from the year-specific ledger.

4. Levy Calculator — Top Cards Not Updating on Year Change

Bug: The "Total Annual Budget", "Admin Fund", and "Sinking Fund" cards on the Levy Calculator tab always showed the 2026 figures, regardless of the selected year.

What was fixed:

  • The backend now returns year-specific budget figures for every year.
  • The trust account override (which stores the current year's exact budget) is only applied for the current financial year (2026). Historical years use the recorded annual levy data.
  • The cards now display the Financial Year label (e.g. "FY 2021") so it's clear which year is shown.
  • A loading indicator is shown while data is fetching after a year change.

5. Transactions — 2021–2024 Showing Empty State

Note: Digital transaction records are available from FY 2025 onwards. Years 2021–2024 do not have transaction records entered in the system yet.

The empty state message has been improved to explain this clearly. If historical transaction data is imported in the future, it will appear automatically for those years.

Property Type Correction (TH071–TH087)

All townhouse units (TH001–TH087) were confirmed to have property_type = 'townhouse'. An earlier data migration had incorrectly set TH071–TH087 to 'apartment'. This has been corrected across all historical records.

Civium Portal Data Integration (April 2026 — Update 3)

The Civium strata portal scraper now feeds live data directly into the finance dashboard pages. All portal data is displayed alongside (not replacing) the levy management system — you now have a single view combining both sources.

What's New

  • Finance Overview → Bank Account Balances card — Shows the live admin fund and sinking fund bank balances as reported by the Civium portal, including the last sync time.
  • Collection Rate page → Civium Portal Snapshot — Displays arrears and credit totals (with unit counts), the portal's own collection rate, and a risk level indicator (Green / Amber / Red).
  • Unit Finance Detail → Portal Cross-Check — For each unit, shows the portal's recorded balance and status alongside the levy system balance so discrepancies are immediately visible.
  • Spending Categories → Portal Actual column — The budget table now includes a "Portal Actual" column showing the real expenditure as reported by Civium, making it easy to compare budget against actuals.

Important: Two Systems, One View

The portal data (Civium) and the levy management system are kept as separate sources of truth:

  • The levy system (unit_levy_ledger, annual_levies) is the authoritative source for billing, levy notices, and payment history.
  • The portal snapshot (strata_owners, bank_accounts) is the authoritative source for bank balances, portal-reported arrears, and actuals from Civium.
  • Neither overwrites the other — data is joined at display time, never copied between systems.

When Portal Data Is Not Available

If the portal scraper has not yet run for a building, the portal cards and columns simply do not appear. All existing financial data and functionality is unaffected. Portal sections are visible only once the scraper has completed at least one successful sync.
